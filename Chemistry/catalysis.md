# Catalysis 
A catalyst is a substance that can increase or decrease the rate of a chemical reaction but itself doesn't take part in a reaction. The whole phenomenon is known as catalysis. 

$$
KClO \underset{700-800 K}{\overset{MnO_2}{\rightarrow}} KCl + 3/2 O_2
$$

- Based on the rate of reaction, catalyst can be classified as,
    1. **Positive Catalyst:** increase the rate of reaction, e.g., $MnO_2$ in deposition of $KClO$
    2. **Negative Catalyst:** decreases the rate of reaction, e.g., $H_3PO_4$ in decomposition of hydrogen peroxide into water and oxygen water and oxygen, $H_2O_2 \overset{H_3PO_4}{\rightarrow} H_2O + 1/2 O_2$
    3. **Auto-Catalyst:** an auto-catalyst is a substance formed during a chemical reaction that increases the rate of the same reaction by acting as catalyst. E.g.: in the reaction between potassium permanganate and oxalic acid, the $Mn^{2+}$ ion formed acts as auto-catalyst.

## Promoter
- Substances that increase the activity of catalyst are called promoters. 

- E.g.: in Habor's process of manufacturing of ammonia. 
    - $N_2(g) + 3H_2 \overset{Fe (s)}{\underset{Mo}{\rightarrow}} 2NH_3 (g)$ 

## Catalytic Poison / Inhibitor 
- Substances that decrease the activity of a catalyst are called catalytic poison or inhibitor. 
    - E.g.: $SO_2 (g) + 1/2 O_2 (g) \underset{As_2S_3}{\overset{Pt}{\rightarrow}} SO_3 (g)$
        - Here, $Pt$ is the catalyst and $As_2S_3$ is the inhibitor. 

Based on the phase (solid/liquid/gas), catalysis can be classified as: 

1. **Homogeneous Catalysis:** when the catalyst and reactants are in same phase, the phenomenon is known as Homogeneous Catalysis. 
    - $SO_2 (g) + 1/2 O_2 (g) \overset{NO}{g} SO_3$

2. **Heterogeneous Catalysis:** > when the catalyst and reactants are in different phase, the phenomenon is known as heterogeneous catalysis.
    - $N_2 (g) + 3H_2 (g) \overset{Fe(s)}{\Rightarrow}2NH_3$

## Enzyme Catalyst 
Enzymes are biological catalyst that help in metabolism and catabolism. The increase in rate of a reaction by an enzyme is called enzyme catalysis. 

- E.g.: Conversion of cane sugar into glucose and fructose by invertase.  
- E.g.: decomposition of glucose into ethyl alcohol and carbon dioxide. 
    - $C_6H_{12}O_6 \overset{\text{zymase}}{\rightarrow} C_2H_5OH + CO_2$

### Characteristics of Enzyme Catalyst 
1. They are highly efficient. 
2. Highly specific in nature. 
3. Highly active in optimum pH (5-7). 
4. Highly active in optimum temperature (298 K - 310 K).

## Industrial Application of Catalyst 
1. **Manufacturing of Ammonia by Habor's Process**
    - $N_2 + 3H_2 \overset{Fe (s)}{\underset{Mo}{\leftrightarrows}} 2NH_3 + \text{heat}$
    - This reaction is exothermic, therefore according to Le Chatelier's Principle, it is favorable at low temperature. However, the rate of reaction is very low at low temperature that is industrially not feasible. Therefore, we use iron catalyst and molybdenum to promote the reaction. 

2. **Manufacturing of Sulfuric Acid by Contact Process**
    - $SO_2 + 1/2 O_2 \overset{N_2O_5}{\leftrightarrows} SO_3 + \text{heat}$
    - In the manufacturing of $H_2SO_4$ by contact process, initially $SO_3$ is formed. This reaction is favorable at low temperature, but then the rate is lowered. Therefore, we use $V_2O_5$ catalyst to speed up the reaction. 

## Catalytic Cracking (Cracking in Industry)
It is an industrial process in whech large hydrocarbon molecules are broken down to smaller units. Earlier, this process was done at high temperature and pressure without catalysts. Modern cracking uses **zeolite** as catalyst.  
Zeolites are 3-dimensional complex **alumino-silicates**.

E.g.: $C_{15}H_{32} \overset{\text{Zeolite}}{\rightarrow} 2C_2H_4 + C_3H_6 + C_2H_18$

### Advantages of Catalytic Cracking 
1. It is carried at low temperature and pressure.
2. Relatively more economically stable coke formed. 
3. More selective cracking and less bi-products.
