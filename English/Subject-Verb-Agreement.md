# Subject-Verb Agreement
- Subject and verb agrees under 2 parameters: 
    1. Number 
    2. Person 

| Subject | Verb | 
|:-:|:-:|
| Singular | Singular | 
| Plural | Plural | 

| Subject | Verb | 
|:-:|:-:|
| 1st person | 1st person | 
| 2nd person | 2nd person |
| 3rd person | 3rd person |

E.g.: His knowledge of many languages **is** remarkable.  
E.g.: The results of his annual examination **were** bad. 

- **Subject word:** the most significant word in a many-word subject that determines the form of the verb. 

## Exceptions 
1. When two singular nouns are joined by 'and' and forms the subject, the verb must be **plural** but when the two singular noun refer to the same person, place, thing or object, the verb must be **singular**.
    - Gold and silver **are** precious metals. 
    - Bread and butter **is** his chief food. 
    - The poet and the novelist **are** dead.

2. Word joined to a singular subject using 'as well as', 'with', 'its', etc., the verb will be **singular**
    - Tea as well as coffee **is** grown in India. 
    - The cow with its calf **is** dead. 

3. Two singular subject connected by or, nor, either, neither, etc. will require a singular verb. 
    - Either Ram or Mohan **is** absent 
    - Neither Sita or Gita **is** here.
    - Either his manager or his servants **are** to be blamed. 

4. When a singular noun and a plural noun is connected by by or, nor, neither, either, etc., then the noun closer to the verb will decide the form of the verb. 
    - Either his servants or his manager **is** to be blamed.

5. Certain collective nouns such as jury, committee, etc. can be used both as singular as well as plural, and therefore they may take any form of verb. 
    - The committee/jury **is/are** divided in opinion. 

6. Certain nouns such as news, innings, etc. are plural in form but singular in meaning, so they will take a singular verb. 
    - The first innings **was** soon over. 
    - No news **is** good news. 

7. Certain common nouns such as fish, furniture, sheep, deer, etc. has no singular form. So they will require a singular verb depending on their use. 
    - The sheep **is** a domestic animal. 
    - The sheep **are/is** grazing in the meadow. 
    - The furniture **is/are** new. 

8. Certain uncountable nouns such as hair, sand, water, etc. are always plural, but they require a singular verb. 
    - Water **is** colorless. 
    - Sand **is** used in construction. 

9. Certain nouns such as scissors, glasses, pants, shoes, socks, etc. are always plural and require a plural verb but if we use them as a pair, they become singular and therefore will require a singular verb. 
    - His socks **are** wet. 
    - His shoes **are** new. 
    - His pair of socks **is** wet. 
    - My pair of shoes **is** new.  

10. If a proper noun refers to the name of a specific country or book, they will take singular verb even if they are plural. 
    - The USA **is** a big country. 
    - The **Arabian Nights** is a collection of stories. 

11. If a plural noun refers to specific quantity or amount then the verb must be singular. 
    - One hundred paise **is** a rupee. 
    - Fifty miles **is** a long distance. 
    - Two third of the army **was** lost in the battle. 

12. Words connect to a singular subject using 'with its' will always require a singular verb. 
    - The cow with its calf **is** dead. 

13. 'Here' and 'there' can never be the subject and therefore the subject(s) in the given sentence will decide the form of the verb. 
    - Here she **has** arrived. 
    - There **are** fifteen mangoes in the basket. 
    - There **is** the bus.
