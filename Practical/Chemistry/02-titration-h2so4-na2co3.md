## Aim of the Experiment 
To determine the strength of supplied $H_2SO_4$ solution volumetrically in normality and in g/L by titrating against standard $Na_2CO_3$ solution using methyl orange as the indicator. 

## Apparatus Required 
Burette, pipette, conical flask, burette stand, glaze tiles, etc. 

## Chemicals Used 
Standard $Na_2CO_3$ solution (0.105 N), $H_2SO_4$ solution, methyl orange. 

## Chemical Reaction 
$Na_2CO_3 + H_2SO_4 + CO_2 (\uparrow) + H_2O$

## Theory 
It is an acid-base titration between a strong acid and weak base. Methyl orange is used as the indicator for this titration. It gives pink color in acidic medium, orange in neutral medium and yellow color in basic medium.  
The strength of $H_2SO_4$ solution is calculated by using the Normality equation - 

$$
N_1V_1 = N_2V_2
$$

Here,  
$N_1$ = strength of $H_2SO_4$ solution  
$N_2$ = strength of $Na_2CO_3$ solution 
$V_1$ = volume of $H_2SO_4$ solution 
$V_2$ = volume of $Na_2CO_3$ solution 

The g/L of $H_2SO_4$ solution = $\text{Normality} \times \text{gm. equivalent wt.} = N_1 \times 49$

## Procedure 
1. Fill up the burette with $H_2SO_4$ solution. 
2. Take 25 ml of $Na_2CO_3$ solution in a conical flask. 
3. Add 2-3 drops of methyl orange indicator in it. 
4. Now, titrate this solution with $H_2SO_4$ solution until an orange color gets developed in the solution. 
5. Record the final reading of the burette. 
6. Repeat the same procedure to get minimum of two concordant readings.

## Calculation 
$N_1V_1 = N_2V_2$

$N_1 = \frac{N_2V_2}{V_1}$  
$\implies \frac{0.105 \times 25}{18.5}$  
$\implies 0.1419 N$

$\text{In g/L} = N_1 \times 49$  
$\implies 0.1419 \times 49$  
$\implies 6.95 g/L$

## Result 
The strength of the supplied $H_2SO_4$ is 0.1419 N or 6.95 g/L. 

## Precautions 
1. Always rinse the burette and the pipette with the solution to be taken in them. 
2. Never rinse the conical flask with the experimental solutions. 
3. Remove the air gaps if any, from the burette. 
4. Never use pipette and burette with broken nozzle. 
5. The strength of the solution must be calculated up to the fourth decimal place.
