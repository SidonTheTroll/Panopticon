## Aim of the Experiment 
To determine the strength of supplied $NaOH$ solution volumetrically in normality and in gm/L by titrating against a decinormal solution of $HCl$ by using Phenolphthalein as the indicator. 

## Apparatus Required 
Burette, Pipette, Conical Flask, Burette stand, Glaze tiles, etc. 

## Chemical Used 
0.1 N $HCl$ solution, supplied $NaOH$ solution, Phenolphthalein indicator 

## Theory 
It is an acid-base titration between a strong acid and strong base. The phenolphthalein indicator gives pink color in basic medium and is colorless in acidic and neutral medium.  
The strength of $NaOH$ solution is determined by titrating a definite volume of this solution against standard $HCl$ solution using the phenolphthalein indicator. The strength is calculated by using the normality relationship. 

$$
N_1V_1 = N_2V_2
$$

Here,  
$N_1$ = strength of $NaOH$ solution  
$V_1$ = volume of $NaOH$ solution  
$N_2$ = strength of $HCl$ solution  
$V_2$ = volume of $HCl$ solution  

The $gm/L$ of $NaOH$ solution = $\text{Normality} \times \text{gm. equivalent wt. of } NaOH = N_1 \times 40$

## Procedure 
1. Fill up the burette with a standard $HCl$ solution up to zero level by leveling the lower meniscus of the solution. 
2. Take 25 ml of supplied $NaOH$ solution in a conical flask. 
3. Add 2-3 drops of phenolphthalein indicator in it. 
4. Now place the conical flask under the burette and run $HCl$ solution to it drop by drop with constant stirring of the flask until the pink color changes to colorless solution. 
5. At this stage, stop the titration and record the final reading of the burette. 
6. Repeat the same procedure to get minimum of two concordant readings. 

## Observation

## Calculation 
$N_1V_1 = N_2V_2$ 

$N_1 = \frac{0.1 \times 15.5}{25}$  
$\implies 0.062 N$ 

In g/L = $0.062 \timse 40$  
$\implies 2.48 g/L$

## Result 
The strength of the supplied $NaOH$ solution is 0.062 N or 2.48 g/L. 

## Precautions 
1. Always rinse the burette and the pipette with the solution to the taken in them. 
2. Remove the air gaps if any, from the burette. 
3. Never use pipette and burette with a broken nozzle. 
4. Don't blow out the last drop of the solution from the pipette. 
5. The strength of the solution must be calculated up to the fourth decimal place.
