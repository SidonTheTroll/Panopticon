# Safety Rules for Carpentry Workshop 
## Introduction 
Carpentry and joinery are common terms used for any kind of woodwork. Carpentry deals with constructional work such as roofs, partitions, and other structural components of a building, while joinery involves making doors, windows, cupboards, dressers, and interior furniture.  
Safety is a matter of common sense. It requires only a few precautions on the job. Once the right attitude is developed, safety becomes a habit. Therefore, proper precautions must be taken and the following safety rules should always be followed in the carpentry workshop. 

1. Pay close attention to the task being performed. 
2. Keep the workplace clean and tidy. 
3. Avoid wearing loose clothing in the workshop. 
4. Do not laugh or joke with others while working. 
5. Wear industrial safety shoes to protect your feet. 
6. Never mishandle tools. 
7. Those with long hair should tie it back and wear a cap for safety. 
8. Wear goggles while grinding. 
9. Always keep hand tools in their proper places. 
10. Do not use greasy spanners or hammers, as they may slip and cause injury. 
11. Receive proper instructions from the instructor before starting any machine. 
12. Do not operate a machine without full knowledge of its functions. 
13. Do not use files without handles. 
14. Ensure all guards are in place before operating power tools.

# Carpentry Joints 
**Classification of Joints:** The various joints used in woodworking can be classified as follows:  

## Basic Woodworking Joints
### 1. Framing Construction 
Used in making tables, chairs, cots, and boxes.  
1. Through mortise and tenon joint  
2. Mortise and tenon joint  
3. Half-lap joint or cross half-lap joint  
4. Corner lap joint  
5. Housing joint  

**Framing joints:** These joints are used to connect wooden pieces at desired angles and are commonly employed in framework construction.  

### 2. Widening Joints 
1. Dowelled joint  
2. Tongued and grooved joint  
3. Rebate joint  
4. Tongued and screwed (table top) joint  
5. Cot or bed top surface boards  

**Widening joints:** These are used for joining wood pieces along their sides to increase the overall width.  

### 3. Lengthening Joints 
1. Table screwed joint  
2. Butt scarf joint  
3. Racking or wedge scarf joint  
4. Tabled and wedge scarf joint with plate/post plate  
5. Beam joint  

**Lengthening joints:** These joints are used for connecting shorter pieces of wood to obtain longer lengths.
