## Stretcher Bond
In this arrangement, all the bricks are laid as stretchers. The overlap, usually equal to half a brick, is obtained by commencing alternate courses with a half brick. The stretcher bond is used only for half-brick-thick walls. It is commonly employed in half-brick-thick leaves of cavity walls, partition walls, etc. Since there are no headers, suitable reinforcement should be provided to strengthen the bond.

## Header Bond
In this type of bonding, all the bricks are laid as headers on the face. The overlap, usually half the width of a brick, is achieved by using internally thick mortar in each alternate course of bricks. This bond allows better alignment and is particularly useful for weak curves on a plane. It is primarily used in foundations to ensure better transverse distribution of load.

# Characteristics of Good Bricks
1. **Color:** Bricks should have a uniform color, preferably deep red, cherry red, or copper red.
2. **Size and Shape:** The size should conform to standard specifications, and the shape should be uniform.
3. **Texture:** The texture should be compact and uniform.
4. **Hardness:** Bricks should not be scratched by a fingernail. When two bricks are struck together, they should produce a metallic sound.
5. **Water Absorption:** When immersed in water for 24 hours, a brick should not absorb more than 20% of its weight.
6. **Toughness:** Bricks should have low thermal conductivity and provide sound insulation.
7. **Strength/Impact Resistance:** Bricks should not break when dropped from a reasonable height (typically around 1 meter).
8. **Plastering:** Plastering covers walls with a layer of mortar to improve appearance, correct surface irregularities, and provide a base for whitewashing, painting, or distempering.

## Advantages of Plastering
1. Provides a smooth and finished surface.
2. Protects the structure from atmospheric actions such as sun, wind, and rain.
3. Corrects irregularities of walls.
4. Conceals inferior or uneven construction materials.
5. Provides a base for finishes such as whitewashing, color washing, painting, and distempering.
6. Generally, two types of plastering are used:
   1. Cement Plastering (cement + sand + water)
   2. Lime Plastering (lime + sand + surki + water)

## Types of Bricks
1. **First Class Bricks:** Table-molded bricks with clean, square, and straight edges. They satisfy all essential qualities and are used for superior construction.
2. **Second Class Bricks:** Ground-molded bricks burnt in kilns. Surfaces may not be very smooth, and shapes may be slightly irregular. Used for general brickwork or plastered work.
3. **Third Class Bricks:** Molded and burnt bricks with irregular shapes and rough surfaces. Not very hard and used for temporary or non-structural work.
4. **Fourth Class Bricks:** Oven-burnt bricks with regular shapes but dull surfaces. Used for low-quality or temporary construction where appearance and strength are not critical.
