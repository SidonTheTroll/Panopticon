# Safety 
There is a great importance of safety in one's life. Whatever may be the work, wherever the workman does his job, his life is not safe until he follows the safety rules. For not paying attention towards safety, we have to suffer physically as well as production and economic capacities are also affected. Due to these reasons, workshop gives a great importance to safety measures.   
While working in any field, we have to keep in mind the things which keep our body safe and work is performed easily and accurately. All these things are called safety. 

## Causes of Accidents  
Accidents often occur in workshops due to various reasons. These accidents can cause injuries to workers and, in severe cases, may even result in death. The main causes of accidents are:  

1. Carelessness  
2. Lack of knowledge  
3. Lack of resources  

To prevent accidents, proper safety measures should be followed in every workshop. These measures can be divided into three categories:  

### Self-Safety  
1. Always receive instructions from the instructor before starting any work in the workshop.  
2. Have complete knowledge of the machine you are operating.  
3. Replace any exposed or damaged electrical wires immediately.  
4. Clean the machine and ensure that safety guards are properly placed on pulleys and belts.  
5. Inspect all machine parts before use.  
6. Do not wear loose clothing while operating a machine.  
7. Avoid wearing neckties, rings, or other accessories. Always wear thick, sturdy shoes.  
8. Prevent oil or grease from spilling on the workshop floor.  
9. Keep the shop floor free of scrap and debris.  

### Machine Safety  
1. The operator should be familiar with all parts of the machine and check its condition before starting work.  
2. Do not change levers or gears while the machine is running.  
3. Do not strike the machine bed with a hammer. Use a proper wrench while removing the lathe chuck.  
4. Ensure proper lubrication of the gearbox and all moving parts.  
5. Repair or replace any damaged machine parts immediately.  
6. Be cautious of electrical fluctuations; low voltage may cause the motor to burn out.  
7. Inform the instructor immediately about any irregularities and always follow their instructions.  

### Work Safety  
1. Select the appropriate machine for the specific job. Prepare a work plan and follow it.  
2. Choose the correct machine speed and cutting tool according to the nature of the work.  
3. Hold the tool and workpiece firmly while operating.  
4. Stay alert and focused while working.  
5. Never take measurements while the machine is running.  

### Safety Signs  
A safety sign is a visual indicator referring to a specific object, activity, or situation that provides important information or instructions related to safety.  

## Steel Rule
Steel rules are made of **spring steel** or **stainless steel**. The surface of a steel rule is **satin chrome finished** to reduce glare and prevent rusting.
Steel rules are available in various lengths, the most common being **150 mm, 300 mm, and 600 mm**. The **least count** (smallest measurable division) of a standard steel rule is **0.5 mm**.

The **base unit of linear measurement** according to **BIS (Bureau of Indian Standards)** is the **meter**.

- **Meter:** According to the wavelength standard, one meter is defined as **1,650,763.73 times the wavelength of the radiation corresponding to the transition between levels** $(2p^{10}) \text{ and } (5d^5)$ **of the krypton-86 atom in a vacuum**.

- **Rule:** A rule is a **strip of wood, metal, or other suitable material** graduated into **actual units of measurement**, used to measure the true dimensions of an object.

- **Scale:** A scale is a **strip of wood, metal, or other suitable material** graduated into **representative units**, used to **check or lay out proportional or scaled measurements** on drawings or models.

## Try Square  
The **try square** is a precision instrument used to check the **squareness of a surface**. The accuracy of measurement by a try square is approximately **0.002 mm per 10 mm** of length.  

A try square consists of a **blade** that is fixed to the **stock** at an angle of **90°**. It is made of **hardened steel** and is specified according to the **length of its blade**, typically **100 mm, 150 mm, or 200 mm**.  

### Uses  
- To **check the squareness** of surfaces.  
- To **check the flatness** of a surface.  
- To **mark lines at 90°** on workpieces.  
- To **set workpieces at right angles** on work-holding devices.  


## Hacksaw Frame and Blade  
Hacksaws are used for cutting metals and consist of two main parts — the **frame** and the **blade**. There are two types of hacksaw frames:  

1. **Solid Frame:**  
   - Can accommodate only one specific standard blade length.  
2. **Adjustable Frame:**  
   - Can hold blades of various standard lengths.  

### Hacksaw Blades  
Hacksaw blades are made from either **low alloy steel** or **high-speed steel (HSS)**.  
The **standard lengths** of hacksaw blades are **250 mm** and **300 mm**.  

#### Types of Hacksaw Blades  
1. **All Hard:**  
   - The entire width of the blade between the pin holes is hardened.  
2. **Flexible Blade:**  
   - Only the teeth are hardened; the rest of the blade remains flexible.  
   - These blades are ideal for **cutting along curved lines**.  

### Designation of Hacksaw Blades  
Blades are classified according to their **length**, **pitch**, and **type of tooth set**:  

| Type | Tooth Pitch | Tooth Set Type |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Coarse Pitch | 1.8 mm | Staggered Set |
| Medium Pitch | 1.4 – 1.0 mm | Wave or Staggered Set |
| Fine Pitch | 0.8 mm | Wave Set |

### Saw Tooth Setting  
To prevent the saw blade from binding during cutting and to allow smooth movement, the cut must be **wider than the blade thickness**. This is achieved by **setting the teeth**.  

#### Types of Saw Tooth Settings  
1. **Staggered Set**  
2. **Wave Set**  

## Calipers  

Calipers are simple measuring instruments used to transfer measurements from a steel rule to an object and vice versa.  

Calipers vary based on the **type of joint** and the **shape of the legs**. The most commonly used types are **firm joint** and **spring joint** calipers.  

### Types of Calipers by Joint  
1. **Firm Joint Caliper:**  
   - Both legs are pivoted at one end.  
   - The legs are adjusted manually by applying pressure.  

2. **Spring Joint Caliper:**  
   - The legs are connected by a spring-loaded pivot.  
   - Opening and closing of the legs are controlled by a **screw and nut mechanism**.  
   - These calipers allow **quick and fine adjustments**.  

The **size of a caliper** is specified by the **distance between the pivot center and the tip of the leg**.  
The **accuracy of measurement** depends largely on the operator’s sense of *feel* or *touch*.  

### Types of Calipers by Leg Design  
1. **Outside Caliper:**  
   - Used to measure **external dimensions**, such as the outer diameter of a shaft.  

2. **Inside Caliper:**  
   - Used to measure **internal dimensions**, such as the diameter of a hole or slot.  

3. **Jenny Caliper (Hermaphrodite / Odd Leg / Point Caliper):**  
   - Used for **marking and layout work**.  
   - Can mark **lines parallel to edges** or **locate centers of round bars**.  
   - Ideal for **scribing lines along curved or straight surfaces**.  
   - For accurate settings, the point of the jenny caliper should “click” into the steel rule graduations.  

### General Notes  
- Calipers are used **along with steel rules**, making them **indirect measuring tools**.  
- The **accuracy** is limited to approximately **0.5 mm**.  
- **Parallelism of workpieces** can be checked using an inside caliper.  

## Punches  
Punches are used to make certain dimensional features of a layout permanent by creating small indentations or marks on the workpiece. They are typically made of **high carbon steel** for durability and hardness.  

There are two main types of punches: **center punch** and **prick punch**.  

### Types of Punches  
1. **Center Punch:**  
   - Point angle: **90°**  
   - Used for **locating holes** before drilling.  
   - The wider punch mark provides a good seat for the drill point to start accurately.  

2. **Prick Punch:**  
   - Point angle: **30° or 60°**  
   - The **30° prick punch** is used for making **light punch marks** to position dividers.  
   - The **60° prick punch** is used for marking **witness marks** on layout lines.  
   - Witness marks should not be placed too close to one another to maintain clarity.  

### Other Types of Punches  
- **Hollow Punch:**  
  Used for cutting **cylindrical holes** in **non-metallic materials** such as leather, rubber, or fiber.  
  The front end is hollow, allowing waste pieces to be easily removed.  
  Available in sizes from **1.5 mm to 13 mm**, typically in 1 mm increments.  

- **Bell Center Punch:**  
  Used for **locating the center** of a **cylindrical workpiece**.  
  It can accommodate different diameters, allowing quick and accurate centering.  

- **Pin Punch:**  
  Has a **knurled body and shank** for a firm grip.  
  Used to **drive in or remove** dowel pins or locating pins.  
  Standard sizes range from **3 mm to 12 mm** in diameter and **100 mm to 200 mm** in length.  

- **Letter and Number Punches:**  
  Used to **stamp identifying symbols, letters, or numbers** on metal surfaces.  
  Available in sizes ranging from **0.8 mm to 13 mm**.  

- **Wad Punch:**  
  Similar in function to a hollow punch but used for **larger diameter holes**.  
  After cutting, the waste pieces are removed using a **metal rod** pushed through the hollow section.  

## Hammer  
A **hammer** is a hand tool used primarily for **striking purposes**. The **face** is the striking portion of the hammer and is slightly convex to prevent the edges from digging into the workpiece.  

The **peen** is the opposite end of the hammer head, used for **shaping, riveting, and bending** operations. Both the face and peen are **hardened**, while the **cheek** remains unhardened. The **weight** of the hammer is stamped on the cheek.  

An **eyehole** is provided in the hammer head to **fix the handle**, which is secured firmly using a **wedge**. This ensures a tight and rigid fit.  

Hammers are specified by their **weight** and the **shape of the peen**. Their weight generally ranges from **125 grams to 1.5 kilograms**.  
According to the shape of the peen, there are three main types of hammers:  

1. **Ball Peen Hammer** – Used for riveting, bending, and shaping metal.  
2. **Cross Peen Hammer** – Used for starting panel pins and shaping in corners.  
3. **Straight Peen Hammer** – Used for working in confined spaces and along edges.  

### Before Using a Hammer  
1. Ensure the handle is properly fitted and secured.  
2. Select a hammer of suitable weight for the specific job.  

## Surface Gauges  
A **surface gauge** is one of the most common marking tools used in metalworking and inspection work. It serves several purposes:  

1. **Scribing lines** parallel to a datum surface.  
2. **Setting jobs** on machines parallel to a datum surface.  
3. **Aligning jobs** concentrically with the machine spindle.  

It is also known as a **scribing block** or **marking block**.  

Surface gauges are generally of two types:  
1. **Fixed Type**  
2. **Universal Type**  

### Universal Surface Gauge  
The **universal surface gauge** includes several additional features that enhance its versatility:  

1. The **spindle** can be set in any desired position.  
2. **Fine adjustments** can be made quickly and precisely.  
3. It can be used on **cylindrical surfaces**, as **V-grooves** are provided in the base for stability.  
4. A **guide pin** is provided to help scribe lines parallel to the edge of the workpiece. The guide pin runs along the edge, using it as a **reference surface** for accurate marking.  


## Vice  
A **vice** is a job-holding device used to firmly secure a workpiece during operations such as filing, sawing, drilling, or chipping.  

### Types of Vices  
1. **Bench Vice**  
2. **Machine Vice**  
3. **Hand Vice**  
4. **Leg Vice**  

### Main Parts of a Vice  
1. Fixed Jaw  
2. Sliding Jaw  
3. Jaw Plates  
4. Clamping Bolt and Nut

A vice is **specified by the length of its jaw plates**, typically measured in millimeters or inches.  

## Vernier Caliper  
A **vernier caliper** is a direct precision measuring instrument used to measure internal, external, and depth dimensions accurately. All parts are made of **nickel-chromium steel**, which is **stabilized to prevent distortion** due to temperature variations.  

The working principle of the vernier caliper is based on the **difference between two scales having slightly different divisions**.  

### Accuracy and Least Count  
- The typical accuracy of a vernier caliper is **0.02 mm**, although some models have an accuracy of **0.05 mm**.  
- The **least count** is the smallest measurable value of the instrument.  

### Calculation of Least Count  
The main scale is graduated in millimeters. On the vernier scale, **49 divisions are made equal to 50 divisions on the main scale**.  

$1 \text{ Vernier division} = \frac{49}{50} \text{ mm}$  

$\text{Least Count} = 1 \text{ main scale division} - 1 \text{ vernier scale division}$  
$\implies 1 - \frac{49}{50} = \frac{1}{50} = 0.02\ \text{mm}$  

### Applications  
The vernier caliper is a **universal measuring instrument** used for:  
1. **External measurement** – measuring the outside dimensions of objects.  
2. **Internal measurement** – measuring internal diameters or openings.  
3. **Depth measurement** – measuring the depth of holes, slots, or recesses.  

### Precautions  
- Always ensure the jaws are clean before taking measurements.  
- Avoid excessive pressure while measuring to prevent error.  
- Be aware of **parallax error**, which can occur when reading the coinciding lines from an angle instead of directly above.  


## Micrometer 
Micrometer is a precision measuring instrument. The purpose of a metric micrometer is to read to an accuracy of 0.01 mm of an object. But vernier micrometer's accuracy is 0.001 mm. It is available in various sizes at range of 25 mm. 

Different Types of Micrometers - 

- **Screw Thread Micrometer:** it is used to measure the pitch diameter of the thread. Female piece is fixed on the anvil and male piece is in the spindle. 
- **Tube Micrometer:** a tube micrometer is specially designed to measure the thickness of pipe, tube and other parts of similar shapes. 
- **Digital Micrometer:** this type of micrometer has got a dial at the frame of the micrometer and illuminated screen below it. The advantage of this micrometer is the readings are seen on the screen directly. 
- **Depth Micrometer:** a depth micrometer is designed to measure accurately the depth of grooves, bores, etc. The graduation are in the reversed direction to those of other micrometers. 
- **Flange Micrometer:** a flange micrometer is similar to a regular micrometer and is equiped with two flanges in the place of the anvil and spindle. This is used to measure chordal thickness of gear teeth. 
- **Ball Micrometer:** in this form of micrometer, semi-spherical balls are fitted at the anvil and spindle. It is used to measure a sphere where the point of contact come in between. 

## Lathe 
The machine tool on which turning is carried out is known as Lathe. It is called the father of all machines. Different operations can be performed on lathe machine with the help of accessories such that plain turning, facing, boring, shoulder turning, grooving, drilling, reaming, taper turning, knurling, threading, etc.  
Lathe is a subtraction process machine tool. Because it reduces metal from the original metal. 

- **Turning:** Generally the are two types of turning. Orthogonal turning and Oblique Turning. 
    - **Orthogonal Cutting:** Orthogonal cutting is suitable for cutting operation where only two forces are acting on the tool, which are:
        1. Tangential force 
        2. Axial force 
    - Orthogonal cutting is suitable for finish turning where the depth of cut is less. 
    - Disadvantage is that the start of the cutting the load will act suddengly on the tool. 
    - **Oblique Turning:** Oblique cutting is a process of cutting operation where three forces are acting on the tool, which are: 

1. Axial force 
2. Tangential force 
3. Radial force 

It is suitable for rough turning as the length of the cutting edge is more, load per unit area is less. For the given depth of cut the load is distributed at the start of the cutting operation.  
Disadvantage is that the tool has to be ground for the approach angle. So, more cutting tool material is wasted. 

### Plain Turning 
Plain turning is also known as cylindrical turning or parallel turning. Plain turning is done in two stages: 

1. Rough turning 
2. Finish turning 

By rough turning we bring the size of the work close to the required size, leaving sufficient metal for finishing. But finish turning is done after rough turning, and it aims to bring the size of the work within the specified accuracy and with better surface finish. 

### Facing 
It is an operation of removal of metal from the work face by feeding the tool at right angle to the axis of the work. Facing may be rough and finish. In finish facing, feed rate of 0.05 mm approximately and within a depth of cut not more than 0.1 mm. 

Purpose of Facing: 

1. To have a reference plane 
2. To have a face at right angle 
3. To remove the rough surface 
4. To maintain the length 

Following defects are found on facing operation 

1. **Concave face:** the cause for this defect is the tool digging into the work during the feeding, because the tool is not clamped rigidly. By clamping the tool rigidly with a minimum overhang, this defect may be avoided. 
2. **Convex face:** the cause for this defect is the tool's cutting edge being blunt or the carriage not being locked. To avoid this defect, re-sharpen the tool and use it and also lock the carriage. 
3. **A pip in the center:** this is due to the tool height being below the center height and this defect can be avoided by placing the tool to the center height. 

### Shoulder Turning: 
Wherever more than one diameter is machined on a shaft, the section joining each diameter is called a shoulder or step. The most commonly machined shoulders are: 

1. Square shoulder 
2. Filleted shoulder 
3. Bevelled shoulder 
4. Undercut shoulder 

Purposes of shoulder turning: 

1. For mating parts to fit at right angles against the face of the step. 
2. To eliminate sharp corners and edges. 
3. To give additional strength at the junction of steps. 
4. To give good appearance. 

## Face Groove

### Grooving 
Grooving is the process of turning a grooved form or channel on a cylindrical turned work piece. 

- **Types of grooves:**
    1. Square grooves 
    2. Round groove 
    3. 'V'-shaped groove 

A square groove cut against a shoulder allows a mating part to fit squarely against the shoulder. Round grooves serve the same purpose as square grooves.  
'V' grooves are most commonly found on pulleys driven by 'V' belts.   
A groove may also be cut at the end of a thread to provide a channel into which the threading tool may run.

## Chuck 
Jaw chuck is a job holding device. There are four types of chuck: 

1. 4 jaw chuck 
2. 3 jaw chuck 
3. 2 jaw chuck 
4. Combination chuck 

### 4 Jaw Chuck 
The four jaw chuck is also called as independent chuck. Work can be trued to withing 0.001 to 0.002 mm accuracy. This type of chuck is more heavily constructed than other chucks.  
The parts of a four jaw chuck are: 

1. Back plate 
2. Body 
3. Jaws 
4. Screw 

Body is made of cast iron or cast steel. Jaws are made out of high carbon steel, hardened and tempered. Screws are made out of high carbon steel.  
Regular and irregular jobs can be fastened on a 4-jaw chuck. Some concentric circles are marked on the face of chuck. These circles help to centering a job. 

### 3 Jaw Chuck 
The 3 Jaw chuck is also known as self-centering chuck. Only perfectly round work or work with equally spaces flats, divisible by three, should be held in a 3 jaw chuck. 

Parts of a 3-jaw chuck: 

1. Backplate 
2. Body 
3. Jaws 
4. Crown wheel or scroll plate
5. Pinion 

Crown wheel is made out of alloy steel. 

### 2 Jaw Chuck 
The constructional features of this chuck is are similar to 3 and 4 jaw chucks. Each jaw is an adjustable jaw which can be operated independently. The jaws may be specially machined to hold a particular type of job. 

### Combination Chuck 
The combination chuck is normally a 4-jaw chuck in which the jaws may be adjusted either independently as done is a 4-jaw chuck or used in place where or together as done in a 3-jaw chuck. The chuck is used in places where duplicate work pieces are to be machined. 

Three special chucks are also available: 

1. Collect chuck 
2. Magnetic chuck 
3. Hydraulic chuck or air operated chuck 

Collect chucks are three types: 

1. Push out chuck 
2. Draw in chuck 
3. Dead length bar chuck 

## Tail Stock 
It is a sliding unit on the bed ways of the lathe bed. It is situated on the right-hand side of the lathe. IT is made of two parts - "Base" and "Body".

Purpose of the tail stock. 

1. To accomondate the dead center. 
2. To hold cutting tools like drills, reamers which are provided with taper shank. 
3. To turn the external taper by offsetting the body of the tailstock.
4. To perform external operation on the shaft held between centers. 

## Carriage 
The carriage is the parts of the lathe which slides over the bed-ways between the headstock and tailstock. Carriage mainly consists of two parts: Saddle and Apron. 

Purpose of carriage: 

1. Provides various movements for the cutting tool manually as well as by power feed.
2. Longitudinal feed - provides with the help of carriage movement.
3. Cross-slide - gives the cross-feed.
4. Angular feed - with the help of compound slide movement positioned at an angle to the axis of the work. 

### Cross Slide 
The bottom of the cross-slide has got a dovetail groove machined. The cross-slide is assembled to the saddle with the help of tapered gib. A graduated collar mounted on the screw rod along with the hand wheel helps to set the fine movements of the cross-slide.  
The compound slide- 

1. Swivel base 
2. Top slide 

### Apron
The apron mechanism has the arrangement for transmitting the drive from the feed to the saddle for the longitudinal movement of the tool or the work and the transverse movement of the tool 

### Tool Post 
The tool post holds the tool or tools meant for the operation to be performed on the work. The tool post is assembled to the top slide of the compound rest. The three types of tool posts commonly found on lathes are: 

1. American type tool post or single way tool post 
2. Indexing type tool post or square tool post 
3. Quick change tool post 

Modern lathes are provided with quick change tool post. 

### Bed 
The two function of lathe bed are: 

1. To lockte the fixed units in accurate relationship to each other. 
2. To provide the sideways upon which the operating units can be moved. 

Beds are made of chose grained grey cast iron. For absorbing shock and vibration, the bed should be of considerable weight. Ribs are provided for additional strength. 

The beds are classified according to the shape of the ways. They are 

1. Flat bed 
2. 'V' bed 
3. Combination bed 
4. Gap bed. 

The bed ways are fine-finished by grinding and some have their bed-ways hand scraped.
